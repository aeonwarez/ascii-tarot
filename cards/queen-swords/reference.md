# Queen of Swords

Definitive working reference. Synthesizes all project sources for this court
card; built on the XVII - The Star sheet template, adapted for courts (no
numeral plaque, elemental title band). Unlike most courts, a Harris card scan
DOES exist for this one, so the palette section is observed, not text-derived.

## Element ranking (art priorities)

Tier 1 - the card IS these:
1. **Enthroned upon the clouds** - "She is enthroned upon the clouds." Water of
   Air made regal: she sits above a vast bank of cumulus. The cloud mass is the
   single largest form and the compositional stage; she does not sit on a solid
   throne but rides the air made water. In the scan the clouds are a huge
   cream-white cumulus bank filling the lower half.
2. **The upright sword in her RIGHT hand** - "In her right hand, she bears a
   sword." Held vertical, drawn, the blade of discretion and reason that
   severed the head. This is the rigid vertical read of the card.
3. **The newly severed bearded head in her LEFT hand** - "in her left hand, the
   newly severed head of a bearded man." The head hangs low from her lowered
   left hand, eyes closed peacefully, the face in the trance of deep meditation
   (DuQuette: the Hermit of Virgo perhaps). She has cut the higher intellect
   free of the lower animal soul: "the Liberator of the Mind."
4. **The child's-head crest streaming rays of light** - "Her helmet is crested
   by the head of a child, and from it stream sharp rays of light, illuminating
   her empire of celestial dew." A winged child's head atop the helmet, sharp
   rays radiating out. In the scan these rays read as a green radiant burst
   behind and above her head.

Tier 2 - named and emphasized:
5. **The helmet** - she is helmed (martial Air), the crest set upon it. Not bare
   hair; a war helm crowned by the child's head.
6. **Naked upper body, gleaming belt, and sarong** - "The upper part of her body
   is naked, but she wears a gleaming belt and a sarong." Bare above, belted at
   the waist, draped below in a sarong. In the scan the drapery reads gray-blue.
7. **Empire of celestial dew** - the whole realm she lights is "celestial dew,"
   the watery part of Air made visible: mist, dew, the promise of rain.

Tier 3 - supporting:
8. **Clear conscious perception of Idea** - "She is the clear, conscious
   perception of Idea, the Liberator of the Mind." Her face and pose read keen,
   perceptive, exact; graceful in balance (Libra) and swift in discrimination
   (Virgo / the sword).
9. **Grace and balance** - "Her movements will be graceful, and her ability in
   dancing and balancing exceptional." The Libran poise: a figure held in
   perfect equilibrium above the clouds, not a rigid warrior.

Tier 4 - fine detail, as space allows:
10. **Cumulus that threatens** - DuQuette: Water of Air suggests clouds that
    promise either life-giving rain or the threat of a torrential cloudburst;
    the loaded, latent weather under her.

## Meanings

**Crowley's character description (BoT chapter, canonical):** the person should
be intensely perceptive, a keen observer, a subtle interpreter, an intense
individualist, swift and accurate at recording ideas; in action confident, in
spirit gracious and just. Her movements graceful, her ability in dancing and
balancing exceptional.

**Ill-dignified (BoT):** these qualities all turned to unworthy purposes: cruel,
sly, deceitful and unreliable; very dangerous on account of the superficial
beauty and attractiveness that distinguish her.

**Core esoteric read (BoT):** "She is the clear, conscious perception of Idea,
the Liberator of the Mind." She has used the sword of discretion and reason to
separate the higher faculties of the intellect from the lower nature.

**Yi King (BoT):** 28th hexagram Ta Kwo - the shape suggests a weak beam. The
character, excellent in itself, cannot support interference. Foresight and
prudence safeguard; advantage comes from reliance on help from apparently
unsuitable comrades; this alien strength supplies the defeat of inherent
weakness and may even create superiority to circumstance. Rash adventures may
tempt, foredoomed to failure, but no blame is incurred if True Will is
satisfied. Such people acquire intense love and devotion from the most
unexpected quarters.

**DuQuette (character read):** this is Crowley's own Sun sign and so the most
intimidating card in the deck; one look tells us she means business. Water of
Air suggests clouds that promise either life-giving rain or a torrential
cloudburst. The severed head is the classic Golden Dawn image and a Qabalistic
statement: Swords is Yetzirah, the formative world, and its human counterpart is
the Ruach, the intellect centered in the head. With the sword of discretion she
separates the higher intellect from the Nephesh (animal soul): the Liberator of
the Mind. The bearded head, eyes closed, face in deep meditation, is the Hermit
of Virgo perhaps. Fellow Queens of Swords: Gandhi, Eleanor Roosevelt, H. G.
Wells, Stephen King.

**As a person (court-card use):** anyone whose Sun or rising sign falls in her
zodiacal span (late Virgo into Libra) may carry her traits. Crowley: a person
born on 12th October might possess many of her qualities. She is someone's
birthday card first, an abstraction second.

## Observed Harris palette (from the card scan)

`reference/queen-swords-card.jpg`. Scan source: esotericmeanings.com court-cards
page. A Harris scan of this court card DOES exist, so this palette is observed
from the painting, not merely text-derived:

- **Field / sky:** streaked mid sky-blue with a paler wash, fine pale crackle
  lines running through it (DuQuette confirms "sky blues ... dominate this
  card").
- **Cloud bank: cream-white cumulus** filling the lower half, the largest mass;
  soft-lit, rounded, voluminous.
- **Crest rays: green radiant burst** behind and above the head, the sharp light
  from the child's-head crest reading green-toned.
- **Figure: pale flesh** upper body (naked above the belt), calm and lit.
- **Drapery / sarong: gray-blue**, belt gleaming lighter at the waist.
- **Sword:** pale steel / light gray, held upright.
- **Severed head: small, greenish-toned**, hung low from the left hand.
- **Overall read (DuQuette):** "Sky blues, yellows, and white cumulus clouds
  dominate this card." Keep the palette cool: sky-blue, cream-white cloud,
  gray-blue drape, green crest-rays; touches of pale yellow light. Introduce no
  color with no referent in the scan or text.
