# Prince of Disks

Definitive working reference. Synthesizes all project sources; mirrors the
depth of the trump sheets, adapted for a court card (no numeral, elemental
title, and a Harris scan IS available for this one, so the palette section
is read off the painting).

## Element ranking (art priorities)

Tier 1 - the card IS these:
1. **The ox-drawn chariot.** "His chariot is drawn by an ox, this animal being
   peculiarly sacred to the Element of Earth." He is Vau, the Son "represented
   as in a chariot, going forth to carry out the combined Energy of his
   parents." In the scan the great red bull is planted head-down at the base,
   filling the lower third; the chariot is the card's spine and it is CARRIED,
   not standing. Not the fire-wheel of the Prince of Wands: a slow, heavy,
   earthbound beast.
2. **The orb-globe of geometry in his LEFT hand.** "In his left hand he holds
   his disk, which is an orb resembling a globe, marked with mathematical
   symbols as if to imply the planning involved in agriculture." An armillary /
   wire-frame sphere of latitude-and-longitude rings, not a solid coin. His
   single most identifying object: the Disk made abstract, a revolving
   living symbol (Crowley insists "every Disk is a living and revolving symbol").
3. **The cross-topped orbed sceptre in his RIGHT hand.** "In his right hand he
   bears an orbed sceptre surmounted by a cross, a symbol of the Great Work
   accomplished." Dark shaft, a small orb, a cross finial. Get the handedness
   right: globe LEFT, cross-sceptre RIGHT. It stands for his function, to bring
   forth from matter "that vegetation which is the sustenance of the Spirit
   itself."
4. **The bull-crowned helmet.** "His helmet is crowned with the head of a
   bull." Light armour on the body, a bull's-head crest on the helm that rhymes
   with the ox drawing the chariot below. Two bulls, high and low, frame the
   figure. His eyes are closed in meditation (DuQuette): the whole face is
   inward, "mentally directing the brooding fecundity of the entire universe."
5. **The ploughed, seed-heavy fertile ground.** DuQuette: he is "seated in a
   chariot filled with globular seeds that seem to be ready to burst into
   plants at any moment." The scan is packed with clustered spheres, plough
   furrows, and ripe vegetable masses. The whole field is teeming germination,
   the florescence and fructification of Earth. Fill it; do not leave the
   chariot empty.

Tier 2 - named and emphasized:
6. **Light armour, meditative stillness.** "Clothed in light armour"; the
   figure is "meditative," "the element of Earth become intelligible." Unlike
   the restless bare-armed Prince of Wands, this Prince is held, cool, inward.
   The stillness IS the reading: great energy poised on solid practical matter.
7. **The living, revolving green Disk.** Crowley's new doctrine: "the primary
   colour of Earth not black, but green; it insists that every Disk is a living
   and revolving symbol." Even in the scan's dark browns, the Earth is fertile
   and turning, not dead mineral.

Tier 3 - supporting:
8. **Gnomes / elemental Earth suggestion.** He is "Prince and Emperor of the
   Gnomes"; where the seed-field has room a gnome or delving-figure can be
   implied in the loam.
9. **Taurus, the fixed steady force.** The Prince dominates Taurus, Venus's
   house with Luna exalted; the transition Aries-to-Taurus softens the card
   from Aries's onset to Taurus's patient fertility. Slow, steady, enduring,
   not the Knight's lightning.

Tier 4 - fine detail, as space allows:
10. **Mathematical rings on the orb.** The latitude/longitude and geometric
    marks on the globe (the "planning involved in agriculture"); thin ring
    arcs distinguish it from a plain sphere.

## Meanings

**Crowley's character portrait (BoT chapter; court cards have no separate
divinatory appendix, the chapter description IS the meaning):** great energy
brought to bear upon the most solid of practical matters. Energetic and
enduring, a capable manager, a steadfast and persevering worker. Competent,
ingenious, thoughtful, cautious, trustworthy, imperturbable; constantly seeks
new uses for common things and adapts circumstances to his purposes in a slow,
steady, well-thought-out plan. Lacking almost entirely in emotion, somewhat
insensitive, may appear dull but is not; may often appear stupid and is
inclined to be resentful of more spiritual types. Slow to anger, but if driven
becomes implacable.

**Ill-dignified (BoT):** it is "not very practicable to distinguish between the
good and evil dignities in this card"; in the ill-dignified case "both the
quality and quantity of his characteristics are somewhat degraded." DuQuette
(GD meanings): selfish, animal and material, stupid; in either case slow to
anger but furious if roused.

**GD keyword (DuQuette):** "Increase of matter. Increases good or evil,
solidifies; practically applies things. Steady; reliable."

**As a person (DuQuette shortcut):** somebody's birthday card, April 11 to
May 10. DuQuette: "The Prince of Disks is a devil . . . But what a devil!"
These fellows "change the world with their great energy brought to bear upon
the most solid of practical matters." Named Princes of Disks by Sun sign:
Marcus Aurelius, William Shakespeare, Catherine the Great, Thomas Jefferson,
Ulysses S. Grant, Sigmund Freud, Karl Marx, Robert Oppenheimer, Harry Truman,
Golda Meir. The "garden variety" is the ultimate handyman, cool and in control
on the material plane, eyes closed, directing the brooding fecundity of the
world.

**Vau / the Son:** "the active issue of their union, and its manifestation;
the intellectual image of their union." His action is more enduring than the
Knight's or Queen's. Also "the Dying God, redeeming his Bride in the hour, and
by the virtue, of his murder."

**I Ching correspondence (BoT):** the airy part of Earth is the 53rd Hexagram,
Kien, the flight of wild geese "gradually approaching the shore," then "the
large rocks," "the dry plains," "the trees," "the high," finally "the large
heights." It symbolizes slow, steady emancipation from repressive conditions.

## Observed Harris palette (from the card scan)

`reference/prince-disks-card.jpg` (esotericmeanings.com court-cards page). The
painting is the darkest card of the suit; text and scan agree that Earth here
is rich brown and green, not the "black" of old doctrine:
- Field: warm dark browns and olive-greens, densely packed with clustered
  spheroid seeds and vegetable masses filling the whole frame edge to edge
- **The ox/bull: deep terracotta red-brown**, planted head-down at the base,
  the single most saturated mass; horns and muzzle in darker umber
- **The prince: warm flesh/ochre**, light armour reading as pale bronze-grey,
  bull-crested helmet in muted steel, eyes closed
- **Orb-globe (left hand): pale wireframe** rings against the dark field, the
  one cool light note; the cross-sceptre (right) is a dark near-black shaft
- Chariot and surround: charcoal-to-bronze machined wheels and dark metallic
  arcs behind, ringing the seed-mass
- Border: warm ochre/brown deco frame, bull-head crest sigils
Map for ANSI: browns / umber / olive-green across the whole field; terracotta
red for the ox; ochre flesh and pale bronze for the prince and his armour; a
single pale wireframe note on the globe; dark near-black on the cross-sceptre.
Introduce no color with no referent in the scan (no blue field, no fire red).
