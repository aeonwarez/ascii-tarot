# Queen of Disks

Definitive working reference. Synthesizes all project sources for this court
card; built on the XVII - The Star sheet template, adapted for courts (no
numeral plaque, elemental title band). A Harris card scan DOES exist for this
one, so the palette is observed, not text-derived.

## Element ranking (art priorities)

Tier 1 - the card IS these:
1. **Enthroned queen, throned upon the life of vegetation** - "The Queen of
   Disks is throned upon the life of vegetation." She is the Water of Earth
   made regal: passive "in its highest aspect," the ambition of matter to
   take part in the Great Work. She is the largest mass and the compositional
   stage, but she is turned inward, contemplating the background.
2. **Helmet of the great markhor horns** - "her helmet is adorned with the
   great spiral horns of the markhor." These enormous ram-like spiral horns
   are the single most striking read in the painting: they curl up and out
   above her head, dominating the top third of the card. DuQuette Crest:
   winged goat's head. Not a small crown; a sweeping horned headdress.
3. **The goat upon a sphere before her** - "Before her stands a goat upon a
   sphere. There is here a reference to the dogma that the Great Work is
   fertility." A live goat balanced on an orb, low and forward of the throne.
   The Capricorn beast; the generative, fertilizing sign made animal.
4. **Armour of small scales or coins** - "Her armour is composed of small
   scales or coins." She is armoured in disks, the suit itself worn as mail;
   the scales double as the coins of the Disks suit. DuQuette: "Her dress
   alone is worth looking at with a magnifying glass."

Tier 2 - named and emphasized:
5. **Sceptre surmounted by a cube with a three-dimensional Hexagram** - "in
   her right hand she bears a sceptre surmounted by a cube, within which is a
   three-dimensional Hexagram." Held in the RIGHT hand. The cube is the solid
   of Earth; the interlaced hexagram inside it is the Great Work in three
   dimensions. DuQuette simplifies the symbol to "Scepter with golden orb."
6. **Her proper disk, a sphere of loops and circles interlaced** - "in her
   left arm is curved her proper disk, a sphere of loops and circles
   interlaced." Cradled in the crook of her LEFT arm; a woven, tangled orb of
   coils, not a plain coin. The suit token made intricate.

Tier 3 - supporting:
7. **The barren land turning fertile: river, desert, oases** - "She
   contemplates the background, where a calm river winds through a sandy
   desert to bring to it fertility. Oases are beginning to shew themselves
   amid the wastes." The whole meaning of Water of Earth: dead matter
   receiving life. The river snakes through the waste; green oases begin.
8. **Light falls on only one side of her face** - DuQuette Symbols: "Light
   falls on only one side of her face." Half her face is in shadow; she is
   in-drawn, contemplative, not outward-facing. Dark hair, dark eyes.

Tier 4 - fine detail, as space allows:
9. **The reticent, passive stillness** - "She represents passivity, usually
   in its highest aspect." Quiet, hard-working, domesticated on the surface;
   underneath, "the ambition of matter to take part in the great work of
   Creation." Render her still and self-contained, a mother-force at rest,
   not a queen making a display.

## Meanings

**Crowley's character description (BoT chapter, canonical):** persons signified
possess the finest of the quieter qualities. Ambitious, but only in useful
directions. Immense funds of affection, kindness, and greatness of heart. Not
intellectual and not particularly intelligent, but instinct and intuition are
more than adequate for their needs. Quiet, hard-working, practical, sensible,
domesticated; often (in a reticent, unassuming fashion) lustful and even
debauched, inclined to the abuse of alcohol and of drugs, "as if they could
only realize their essential happiness by getting outside themselves."

**Passivity in its highest aspect (BoT):** "She represents passivity, usually
in its highest aspect." Not inertia but the receptive, mothering function of
Earth: matter made willing to be worked. Ill-dignified, they are dull,
servile, foolish, drudges rather than workers; life becomes purely mechanical,
and they cannot rise or even seek to rise above their appointed lot.

**Crowley's keynote line (BoT):** "She thus represents the ambition of matter
to take part in the great work of Creation." The dogma the card carries: the
Great Work is fertility.

**Yi King (BoT):** 31st hexagram Hsien, meaning Influence; the commentary
describes the effect of moving various parts of the body, from the toes to the
jaws and tongue. The general advice is to go forward quietly without overt
attack upon existing situations.

**DuQuette (character read):** Jane Austen is his ideal model, quietly passive
yet with far more beneath the surface than needlepoint and country dances. He
argues Crowley "was having us on" about her lack of intelligence; the real key
is "the ambition of matter to take part in the great work of creation," which
"is a force to be reckoned with." Water of Earth manifests across a huge range:
strong and charismatic like Joan of Arc, ruthless and manipulative like
Catherine of Aragon, intuitive and superior in intelligence like Newton or
Hawking, a giant of creative force like Beethoven or Tolkien.

**As a person (court-card use):** anyone whose Sun or rising sign falls in her
zodiacal span (roughly late Sagittarius into Capricorn, December 13 to
January 9) may carry her traits. She is someone's birthday card first, an
abstraction second.

## Observed Harris palette (from the card scan)

`reference/queen-disks-card.jpg` (scan source: esotericmeanings.com
court-cards page). The painting confirms and sharpens the text:
- **Foreground dominant: dark greens and rich browns** (DuQuette: "Dark greens
  and rich browns dominate the foreground of this card") - the sweeping green
  blades / vegetation framing left and right read almost black-green.
- **Markhor horns: warm tan / ochre**, spiraling, the brightest structural
  read at the top; banded like real horn.
- **Armour of coins: golden-yellow, tessellated** - the scaled coin-mail over
  her torso is a honeycomb of warm gold, the magnifying-glass detail DuQuette
  praises.
- **Skin / face: warm ochre, lit on one side only** - hard chiaroscuro, half
  the face in green-brown shadow; hair and eyes dark.
- **Goat: pale cream / bone white**, standing forward and low, contrasting the
  dark ground; the sphere beneath it muted.
- **Sceptre: a slim dark shaft** rising at her left (viewer's left), with the
  cube/orb head.
- **Background: paler, cooler** - the sandy desert and winding river open in
  muted grey-tan behind her, greener where the oases begin, so the barren-to-
  fertile gradient reads across the field.
- **Border:** pale warm gray art deco frame consistent with the deck; goat /
  Capricorn and He attributions belong on the title bar (site chrome).
