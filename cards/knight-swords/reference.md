# Knight of Swords

Definitive working reference. Synthesizes all project sources. Follows the
`cards/17-star/reference.md` template (which was written as the template for
all 78 sheets) and the already-done court sheet `cards/knight-wands/reference.md`.
NOTE: this is a COURT card. Unlike the wand court, a Harris scan IS available for
this card in the repo (`reference/knight-swords-card.jpg`, from the
esotericmeanings.com court-cards page), so the palette section below is
observed from the painting, cross-checked against the text.

## Identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Rank / suit | Knight of Swords (Thoth "Knight" = the mounted King figure, the top court rank) |
| Element | Fire of Air (the fiery part of Air; "he is the wind, the storm") |
| Tetragrammaton | Yod of YHVH (Yod subdivision within the suit of Air; the most sublime, original, active part of the Element) |
| Astrology | Rules 21 degrees Taurus to 20 degrees Gemini (last decan of Taurus + first two decans of Gemini); May 11 to June 10 |
| Natural force | The Wind, the storm, the Tempest (Knights = Yod = swift, violent, transient action of the Element; in Air the Knight corresponds to Wind) |
| Original GD titles | "The Lord of the Winds and Breezes; The King of the Spirits of Air; King of the Sylphs and Sylphides" |
| Rules (small cards) | 7 of Disks; 8 of Swords; 9 of Swords |
| Yi King | 32nd hexagram, Hang (long continuance; perseverance in well-doing, continuously acting out the law of one's being) |
| Central figure | A helmed warrior on a maddened steed, driving down the Heavens as the Spirit of the Tempest, sword in one hand, poniard in the other |

Note on the degree phrasing: Crowley's chapter says the Knight "rules from the
21st degree of Taurus to the 20th degree of Gemini." DuQuette's attribution box
rounds this to "20 degrees Taurus to 20 degrees Gemini." These describe the same
span (the rulership begins just past the 20th degree of Taurus). This sheet uses
Crowley's phrasing, 21 Taurus to 20 Gemini, per the primary source.

Note on the Gemini/mutable remark: DuQuette lists the Knight of Swords under the
mutable sign Gemini in his "quadrants of time" table (each Knight dominates a
mutable sign; the Knight of Swords dominates Gemini and adopts the last decan of
the preceding fixed sign Taurus). That is the dominance/decan mechanic, the same
fact as the 21 Taurus to 20 Gemini rulership, not a second attribution.

## Element ranking (art priorities)

Tier 1 - the card IS these:
1. **The rushing warrior diving down the Heavens.** "Mounted upon a maddened
   steed, he drives down the Heavens, the Spirit of the Tempest." The whole card
   plunges on a steep forward/downward diagonal. He is the wind and the storm
   made a rider. Draw the figure head-first and headlong, not upright, a body
   thrown down the sky. This is the #1 compositional read.
2. **The revolving wing crest / the fan of wings.** "for his crest he bears a
   revolving wing." DuQuette: what look at first like four propeller blades
   spinning atop his pointed helmet are actually four transparent, veined
   dragonfly-style wings sprouting from him (perhaps two moving so fast they read
   as four). This spinning wing-fan is the signature of the whole suit of Swords
   and the loudest single shape on the card. Draw it as a whirling angular fan,
   veined and translucent, not a solid block.
3. **The sword and the poniard.** "In one hand is a sword, in the other a
   poniard." Two blades, not one. He represents the idea of attack. Give him both
   weapons: a long sword and a shorter dagger, driven forward with the plunge.
4. **The maddened steed.** "Mounted upon a maddened steed." DuQuette's symbol
   list names a winged brown horse. The mount is carried in the dive beneath the
   rider, a brown mass driven downward through the tempest, not a calm horse.

Tier 2 - named and emphasized:
5. **He rides the wind / the tempest field.** He IS "the wind, the storm... the
   violent power of motion applied to an apparently manageable element." The air
   is not neutral behind him; it is the driving medium he embodies. Rake the
   whole field into streaming wind-lines on the plunge diagonal.
6. **Driving clouds driven before him.** DuQuette symbol list: "driving clouds."
   The sky is full of raked cirrus streaks torn along his path, the storm he is.
7. **The forward/downward plunge rakes everything.** As with the Knight of Wands
   blast, one consistent diagonal governs the card: the wings, the cloud streaks,
   the horse, the blades all lean the same way, thrown by the headlong dive. The
   consistent rake is what turns a warrior-diagram into the Spirit of the Tempest.

Tier 3 - supporting:
8. **Birds / swallows driven before the storm.** Small dark birds scatter ahead
   of and below him, blown before the tempest (visible lower right on the scan).
   Witnesses of the wind, the airy suit's small life driven along.
9. **Green-and-gold scale armour.** He is "a warrior helmed," in complete armour
   as all Knights are (they are the Yod). On the scan the plate reads green and
   gold scale. Dither for metal, not a flat body.
10. **The "extended flame of mind."** Crowley: "the extended flame of mind... the
    True Will exploding the mind spontaneously," a light-shaft of the Ideal.
    Optional: a thin bright shaft or lance of light read through the plunge.

Tier 4 - fine detail, as space allows:
11. **Pointed helm.** The wings spin atop a pointed helmet (DuQuette). A small
    peaked helm crest under the wing-fan.
12. **Dark-brown hair, dark eyes** (DuQuette color note), a small cool detail on
    the exposed face where the visor opens.

## Meanings

**Crowley's court-card description (BoT, canonical):** He represents the idea of
attack. The moral qualities appropriate to this figure are activity and skill,
subtlety and cleverness. He is fierce, delicate and courageous, but altogether
the prey of his idea, which comes to him as an inspiration without reflection. If
ill-dignified, the vigour in all these qualities being absent, he is incapable of
decision or purpose; any action he takes is easily brushed aside by opposition.
"Inadequate violence spells futility. Chimaera bombinans in vacuo."

**Yi King reading (BoT):** The 32nd hexagram, Hang. The meaning is long
continuance, "perseverance in well-doing, or continuously acting out the law of
one's being." Crowley notes this seems incongruous with the Qabalistic idea of
violent energy applied to the least stable element, but the trigram of Air also
indicates wood, suggesting the irresistible flow of the sap strengthening the
tree. He warns via line 6: "shows its subject exciting himself to long
continuance. There will be evil."

**Keynote (DuQuette):** Fire of Air, suggestive of a violent wind. The general
divinatory meaning of the card sums up in one word: attack. Sword air made fiery,
the storm-front of the intellect.

**Crowley's synthesis (BoT):** "the extended flame of mind," the True Will
exploding the mind spontaneously. The influence of Taurus makes for steadiness,
and that of the first decanate of Gemini for inspiration; so picture him "integer
vitae scelerisque purus," a light-shaft of the Ideal absorbing the entire life in
concentrated aspiration, passing from earthy Taurus to exalted Gemini. Here too
is the danger of the symbol: the first decan is the card called "Interference"
(in the old pack, "Shortened Force").

**As a person (Crowley / DuQuette):** any court card pictures the person whose
Sun, or rising sign at nativity, falls within its zodiacal attribution. The
Knight of Swords is the birthday card of those born May 11 to June 10. DuQuette's
example figures: Baron Munchhausen, Arthur Conan Doyle, Pope John Paul II,
Malcolm X, Queen Victoria, John Wayne, John F. Kennedy, Marilyn Monroe, Cher.

**Knight = Yod role (BoT general remarks):** The Knights represent the powers of
the letter Yod in the Name. They are the most sublime, original, active part of
the Energy of the Element, and for this reason are shown on horseback and in
complete armour. Their action is swift and violent, but transient. In the Element
of Air the Knight corresponds to Wind.

## Observed Harris palette (from the card scan)

`reference/knight-swords-card.jpg` (esotericmeanings.com court-cards page). A
scan IS available for this court card, so the palette is observed from the
painting and cross-checked against the color words in the text:
- Field: pale sky blue, streaked top to bottom with white/blue driving cirrus,
  the whole sky raked into wind-lines. DuQuette confirms: "Sky blues, yellows,
  and white cirrus clouds dominate this card."
- **Armour: golden-yellow and green scale** - the plate reads warm yellow-gold
  over green, seen in the plunge; dither for metal volume.
- **Wing crest: pale translucent, veined** - the four fanned dragonfly-style
  wings are near-colorless, faint blue-white with darker vanes, spinning atop the
  pointed helm.
- **Steed: brown / tan** - a warm brown horse mass driven downward beneath the
  rider (DuQuette symbol note: "Winged brown horse," "drawn brown sword").
- **Blades: brownish steel** - sword and poniard read a muted brown-steel, not
  bright silver (DuQuette: "drawn brown sword").
- **Hair: dark brown; eyes: dark** (DuQuette color note).
- **Birds: small dark swallows** driven before the storm, lower right.
- Border: to be set by `tools/frame.py -s swords`; romanized Yod letter and the
  Fire-of-Air element in the title band.

Cross-check note: the text-derived color words (sky blue, yellow, white cirrus,
brown horse, brown sword, dark hair) all agree with the scan; the scan adds the
green in the scale armour that the word list alone does not spell out.
