# Knight of Wands

Definitive working reference. Synthesizes all project sources. Follows the
`cards/17-star/reference.md` template (which was written as the template for
all 78 sheets). NOTE: this is a COURT card, so there is no Harris scan in this
repo (only the 22 trumps are scanned); the palette section is text-derived.

## Identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Rank / suit | Knight of Wands (Thoth "Knight" = the mounted King figure, the top court rank) |
| Element | Fire of Fire (the fiery part of Fire) |
| Tetragrammaton | Yod of YHVH (Yod subdivision of the Yod; the most sublime, original, active part of the Element) |
| Astrology | Rules 21 degrees Scorpio to 20 degrees Sagittarius (last decan of Scorpio + first two decans of Sagittarius); Nov 13 to Dec 12 |
| Natural force | The Lightning flash (Knights = Yod = swift, violent, transient action of the Element) |
| Original GD titles | "The Lord of the Flame and the Lightning; The King of the Spirits of Fire; King of the Salamanders" |
| Rules (small cards) | 7 of Cups; 8 of Wands; 9 of Wands |
| Yi King | 51st hexagram, Kan (the startling, perilous, revolutionary; be apprehensive yet cool, resolute, energetic) |
| Central figure | A warrior in complete armour on a leaping black horse, riding upon flames |

Note on the Aries remark: in the general court theory (BoT p.35) Crowley says
the Knight of Wands "has the attribution of Aries" when discussing the natural
force it represents (swift violence of onset, the lightning flash). That is the
elemental/natural-force reading, not the zodiacal rulership. The precise
zodiacal decan rulership is 21 Scorpio to 20 Sagittarius, per the court-card
chapter and DuQuette. Both are true at once; do not collapse them.

## Element ranking (art priorities)

Tier 1 - the card IS these:
1. **The rearing / leaping black horse.** "His steed is a black horse leaping."
   The whole card explodes upward and forward. DuQuette: the forward thrust of
   mane, tail, hair and beard reads not as jumping but as being blown from
   behind by an unimaginably strong explosive force. Draw it in mid-leap,
   diagonal thrust, a dark dithered mass (not an outline horse).
2. **Riding upon the flames.** "upon the flames does he ride." Fire is not a
   backdrop here, it is the ground. The horse and rider are carried on / out of
   a body of flame. This is the Fire-of-Fire thesis made literal: the mount
   itself rides fire.
3. **The armoured warrior.** "a warrior in complete armour." Knights are clad
   in complete armour BECAUSE they are the Yod, the most active original part
   of the Element. Golden / scarlet-gold armour, seen in violent forward motion.
4. **The flaming torch / club in his hand.** "In his hand he bears a flaming
   torch." (DuQuette lists the symbol as a Club, waving flames.) A raised
   waving flame, the weapon of the suit of Fire.

Tier 2 - named and emphasized:
5. **The black-horse crest on the helm.** "On his helmet for a crest he wears
   a black horse." (DuQuette: winged black horse's head.) The horse doubled:
   the mount below, the winged horse-head crest above. Read the crest even at
   small scale.
6. **The flame in the mantle / cloak.** "a flame also in his mantle." A scarlet
   gold cloak that is itself on fire, streaming behind him in the blast.
7. **The forward blast.** The single strongest read after the horse: everything
   (mane, tail, hair, beard, cloak, flames) is thrown backward by the forward
   thrust, as if the figure is riding a rocket. Rake every trailing element the
   same diagonal.

Tier 3 - supporting:
8. **The winged crest detail.** The wings on the horse-head crest, echoing the
   flight / speed of the whole image.
9. **Beard and streaming hair** (red-gold), part of the blast in directive 7.
10. **The salamander association** (King of the Salamanders) - fire elementals;
    optional flame-tongues licking through the mass as texture.

Tier 4 - fine detail, as space allows:
11. **Grey / hazel eye highlight** on the visor (DuQuette color note).
12. **Scale / plate texture** on the golden armour, dithered so it reads
    metal, not a flat body.

## Meanings

**Crowley's court-card description (BoT, canonical):** The moral qualities
appropriate to this figure are activity, generosity, fierceness, impetuosity,
pride, impulsiveness, swiftness in unpredictable actions. If wrongly energized:
evil-minded, cruel, bigoted and brutal. In either case ill-fitted to carry on
his action; he has no means of modifying it according to circumstances. If he
fails in his first effort, he has no resource.

**Yi King reading (BoT):** The 51st hexagram, Kan. Startling, perilous,
revolutionary events. The Querent is advised to be apprehensive yet cool,
resolute and energetic; to beware of untimely action, but to go forward with
tense confidence in his own ability.

**Keynote (DuQuette):** Fire of Fire. Potentially the strongest member of the
court, but "representing something is not the same as being something." He is
the portrait of someone riding a rocket: aimed right and fueled right it is the
most spectacular of successes; misaimed he misses, under-fueled he crashes,
over-fueled he explodes.

**As a person (Crowley / DuQuette):** any court card pictures the person whose
Sun, or rising sign at nativity, falls within its zodiacal attribution. The
Knight of Wands is the birthday card of those born Nov 13 to Dec 12. DuQuette's
example figures: Robert Fulton, Indira Gandhi, Robert Kennedy, Frank Sinatra,
Mary Queen of Scots.

**Knight = Yod role (BoT general remarks):** The Knights represent the powers
of the letter Yod in the Name. They are the most sublime, original, active part
of the Energy of the Element, and for this reason are shown on horseback and in
complete armour. Their action is swift and violent, but transient. In Fire the
Knight corresponds to the Lightning flash.

## Quotes for the card page

- "He is a warrior in complete armour. On his helmet for a crest he wears a
  black horse. In his hand he bears a flaming torch; a flame also in his mantle;
  and upon the flames does he ride. His steed is a black horse leaping."
  (BoT, Knight of Wands)
- "The Knights represent the powers of the letter Yodh in the Name... In the
  Element of Fire the Knight corresponds to the Lightning flash." (BoT, general
  characteristics of the four dignitaries)
- "Explodes is perhaps a better word... It is the portrait of someone who is
  riding a rocket, and that can be very risky." (DuQuette)

## Observed Harris palette (from the card scan)

`reference/knight-wands-card.jpg` (esotericmeanings.com court-cards page).
The painting confirms and sharpens the text color words:
- **Field: scarlet / vermilion**, overlaid with a burst of pale yellow sun-rays
  behind the figure. The whole card runs hot.
- **Flames: orange and gold** curling waves at the lower right, the ground the
  horse rides out of ("rides upon the flames").
- **Horse: the one dark mass** - deep brown to near-black, rearing on the left
  against the fire (the crest horse-head also dark).
- **Armour: scaled bronze-gold**, warm, catching red light.
- **Cloak / mantle: scarlet**, streaming back on the forward diagonal.
- **Hair and beard: red-gold** (DuQuette color note, confirmed in paint).
- **Read:** a single dark diagonal (horse + rider) driven across a red-and-gold
  furnace; warm on warm except the dark horse. DuQuette's tenor holds: "fiery
  reds, yellows, and gold dominate this card as they do all the Wand court
  cards."
