# Prince of Cups

Definitive working reference. Synthesizes all project sources; mirrors the
depth of the trump sheets, adapted for a court card (no numeral, elemental
title, Harris scan available for this court card).

## Identity

| Field | Value |

| Element | Air of Water ("the airy part of Water": elasticity, volatility, hydrostatic equilibrium; also the catalytic faculty and the energy of steam) |

| Colouring | Hair brown; eyes grey or brown (DuQuette court-detail table) |
| Central figure | A warrior part-clad in armour, enthroned in a shell-shaped chariot drawn by an eagle, lotus in the right hand and serpent-cup in the left |

## Element ranking (art priorities)

Tier 1 - the card IS these:
1. **The eagle-drawn chariot.** "His chariot, which resembles a shell, is also
   drawn by an eagle." The Prince is Vau, the Son "represented as in a chariot,
   going forth to carry out the combined Energy of his parents." In the scan a
   single huge black eagle, wings spread full-bleed left to right, hauls the
   shell chariot straight at the viewer. The eagle is the card's spine and its
   largest mass.
2. **The serpent-cup, borne in the LEFT hand.** "In his left hand is a cup from
   which issues a serpent." A coiled snake rises out of the cup. This is his
   single most identifying object; the eagle is Scorpio's manifested totem, the
   serpent is Scorpio's hidden one, and the cup ties both to Water.
3. **The lotus, borne in the RIGHT hand.** "In his right hand he bears a Lotus
   flower, sacred to the element of Water." DuQuette: "an enormous lotus
   blossom." Balances the serpent-cup; get the handedness right (lotus right,
   cup left).
4. **The prince himself, warrior part-clad in armour that grows rather than
   covers.** "A warrior partly clad in armour, which seems, however, rather a
   growth than a covering." The armour is not a worn shell but an organic
   outgrowth of the body: Air-of-Water made flesh. His helmet is surmounted by
   an eagle (the crest rhymes with the beast drawing him).
5. **The gaseous wings.** "His wings are tenuous, almost of gas. This is a
   reference to his power of volatilization understood in the spiritual sense."
   The wings are barely-there vapor, not solid feather; they read as steam or
   mist rising off the figure. This is the "airy" of Air-of-Water made visible.

Tier 2 - named and emphasized:
6. **The eagle-helmet crest.** "His helmet is surmounted by an eagle." A small
   eagle crest atop the head, echoing the great eagle below. The manifested
   portion of Scorpio "symbolized by the eagle is in reality the least important
   part of his nature," so the crest is emblem, not the card's heart.
7. **The scorpion, present but unshewn.** "The third totem, the scorpion, is not
   shewn in the picture, for the putrefaction which it represents is an extremely
   secret process." The scorpion is the hidden third of Scorpio's triad (eagle /
   serpent / scorpion) and is deliberately absent from the image; honor it as
   the card's secret, not a drawn glyph.

Tier 3 - supporting:
8. **The calm stagnant lake with heavy rain.** "Beneath his chariot is the calm
   and stagnant water of a lake upon which rain falls heavily." A still,
   sinister water surface at the base of the frame, struck by vertical rain. The
   calm surface over hidden depth is the Scorpio mask over intense passion.
9. **The crashing wave.** DuQuette: he "makes a spectacular entrance by bursting
   straight at us from a crashing wave." The chariot surges forward out of water
   in motion, so the base is not only the stagnant lake but a breaking wave
   pushing the whole composition toward the viewer.

Tier 4 - fine detail, as space allows:
10. **The shell chariot body.** The chariot "resembles a shell": a curved,
    ribbed, nautilus-like car cradling the prince, distinct in texture from the
    eagle's feathers and from the growth-armour.

## Meanings

**Crowley's character portrait (BoT chapter; the court cards have no separate
divinatory appendix, so the chapter description IS the meaning):** subtlety,
secret violence, and craft. He is intensely secret, an artist in all his ways.
On the surface he appears calm and imperturbable, but this is "a mask of the
most intense passion." Susceptible on the surface to external influences, he
accepts them "only to transmute them to the advantage of his secret designs."
Thus "completely without conscience in the ordinary sense of the word," and
therefore usually distrusted by his neighbours: they feel they can never
understand him, so he inspires unreasonable fear. "He is in fact perfectly
ruthless. He cares intensely for power, wisdom, and his own aims." He feels no
responsibility to others, and although his abilities are immense, "he cannot be
relied upon to work in harness."

**Ill-dignified (BoT):** the same qualities turned wholly destructive. The
secrecy becomes deceit and betrayal; the calm surface hides pure ruthlessness;
the transmuting intelligence serves only self-interest with no restraint of
conscience.

**As a person (DuQuette shortcut):** somebody's birthday card, October 13 to
November 12. DuQuette believes Crowley "had an unpleasant experience with a
Prince of Cups," since taken literally the card would make one-twelfth of
humanity "melodrama villains." He grants there is "still great strength and
potential in this card": men of immense abilities who "could not be relied upon
to work in harness." Named Princes of Cups by Sun sign: Martin Luther, Oscar
Wilde, Friedrich Nietzsche, Timothy Leary, Pablo Picasso (and the darker Lee
Harvey Oswald, Leon Trotsky).

**Vau / the Son:** "the active issue of their union, and its manifestation; the
intellectual image of their union." His action is more enduring than the
Knight's or Queen's. Also "the Dying God, redeeming his Bride in the hour, and
by the virtue, of his murder."

**I Ching correspondence (BoT):** the airy part of Water is the 61st Hexagram,
Chung Fu, "inmost sincerity / inner truth." "One of the most important figures
in the Yi: it moves even pigs and fish, and leads to great good fortune." A "big
Li" (the trigram of Sol doubled); by shape it suggests a boat, but also the
geomantic figure Carcer, Saturn in Capricornus. "Libra going over into Scorpio
is of tremendous, active, critical energy and weight"; good will, sincerity, and
right mating are the essentials of such people's success, "their danger is
overweening ambition."

## Observed Harris palette (from the card scan)

`reference/prince-cups-card.jpg` (court-cards page, esotericmeanings.com). The
painting confirms and sharpens the text:
- Field: **cool blues and blue-greens dominate** ("as they do the other Cups
  court cards," DuQuette), from pale ice-blue cloud swirls to deep teal water.
- **The prince: green-skinned**, a sea-green nude figure part-clad in
  darker green-blue growth-armour that reads as one continuous surface with the
  body, not a separate metal.
- **The great eagle: near-black / dark slate**, wings barely lighter at the
  primaries, talons pale. It is the darkest mass and the strongest value
  contrast against the pale field.
- **Cup and serpent: warm off-white / bone**, the one small break from the
  blue-green register, so the serpent-cup pops in the left hand.
- **Lotus: pale pink-white with a dark heart**, the second warm accent, in the
  right hand.
- Wings of the prince: **translucent silver-white vapor**, painted as gas, not
  feather.
- Water below: **deep blue-green** lake struck by lighter vertical rain; cloud
  swirls above in whites and pale blues.
- Border: pale warm-gray art deco frame; eagle corner sigils and the court
  elemental title on the band.

Map for ANSI: blue / teal / blue-green across the whole field and water; dark
slate to near-black on the great eagle; sea-green on the figure and its
growth-armour; a bone / off-white serpent-cup and a pale pink lotus as the only
two warm accents; silver-white vapor wings. Introduce no color with no referent
in the scan (no red, no gold field).
