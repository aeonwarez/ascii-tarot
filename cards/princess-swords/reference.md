# Princess of Swords

Definitive working reference for the court card. Synthesizes all project
sources; follows the same structure as the Star sheet, adapted for a court
card (no zodiacal decan). Unlike the Wands princess, a Harris scan IS
available for this card, so the palette section is drawn from the painting.

## Identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Card / name | Princess of Swords (Thoth court, suit of Swords) |
| Attribution | Earth of Air (the earthy part of Air; "the fixation of the volatile") |
| Esoteric titles | "The Princess of the Rushing Winds; the Lotus of the Palace of Air; Princess and Empress of the Sylphs and Sylphides. Throne of the Ace of Swords." (DuQuette) |
| Tetragrammaton | Hé final of the Name; the ultimate issue, crystallization, materialization, and re-absorption of the Energy; "the Silence into which all things return"; an audit of 0=2 |
| Rank meaning | A Throne, not a rider. Together with the Ace of Swords she is its throne; a Princess is set upon her Mother's throne only when the Prince wins her |
| Astrology | NONE. Princesses have no zodiacal attribution. As a throne of Air she rules a quadrant of the heavens (Capricorn/Aquarius/Pisces) around the North Pole, not a decan range |
| Region ruled | With the Ace of Swords, the Capricorn-Aquarius-Pisces celestial quadrant above the North Pole, and the area of the Americas (DuQuette) |
| Yi King | 18th hexagram, "Ku" (troubles): for practical and material matters the most unhappy symbol in the book; the fine qualities of Air weighed down, suppressed, suffocated |
| Central figure | Helmed avenging warrior-maiden with serpent-haired Medusa crest, standing before a barren altar, stabbing downward with a sword; Minerva / Artemis / Valkyrie |

Note on the throne status: Crowley is explicit that the Princesses "have no
Zodiacal attribution" and "represent the He' final of the Name" (BoT lines
5974, 5967). She is "the throne of Spirit" and "may always have the option of
throwing everything overboard, blowing everything sky high" (BoT line 6581).
She rules a quadrant of the heavens around the North Pole (DuQuette line
7765), not a zodiac decan. State this correctly and do not invent a sign or
degree range.

## Element ranking (art priorities)

Tier 1 - the card IS these:
1. **The downward sword-stab** - "she stabs downward with her sword." The
   sword is driven down, not held aloft in salute. The whole figure is bent
   into the descending blow; this is the card's kinetic signature. On the
   scan the blade is raised in both hands over the head at the top of the
   stroke, about to fall on the altar below her.
2. **The barren / profaned altar she avenges** - "She stands in front of a
   barren altar as if to avenge its profanation." A grey stone altar (DuQuette
   plate: "Silver altar, smoke") set low and to one side; her violence is
   directed at it, the destruction of the fixed idea and the profaned form.
3. **The Medusa-helmed head** - "she appears helmed, with serpent-haired
   Medusa for her crest." DuQuette plate: Crest "Medusa's head," "mercifully
   turned away from our view." The helm reads as a mass of writhing serpents;
   the face is turned so it cannot petrify the viewer.
4. **The churning smoke / dust she rises out of** - "The heaven and the
   clouds, which are her home, seem angry." DuQuette: "Rising out of a cloud
   of dust... Swirling clouds of gray-black dust and violent windbursts." She
   stands upon and emerges from a boiling mass of smoke; the field is
   agitated air made visible.

Tier 2 - named and emphasized:
5. **The geometric radiating wings** - Harris gives her stylized angular
   wing-panes fanning out behind the shoulders (DuQuette: "the geometrical
   wings we first encountered on the Princess of Swords" that "festoon the
   backgrounds of the other cards of this suit," line 9324). Faceted, ray-like
   panels, not feathered wings; they read as the fixation of the volatile,
   Air crystallizing into hard geometry.
6. **The fixation of the volatile / materialization of Idea** - the doctrine
   made visible: "She brings about the materialization of Idea... Turning
   things to stone (fixation of the volatile, materialization of Idea) is
   just part of the job description" (DuQuette line 7795). Air (thought)
   hardening into stone; the Medusa gaze and the geometric wings are both
   this same freezing.
7. **The angry heaven behind her** - the sky itself is her home and it is in
   revolt; radiating yellow-green light and dark windbursts, not a calm
   ground.

Tier 3 - supporting / coloring:
8. **Light-brown hair, blue eyes** (DuQuette plate). The single cool point is
   the blue eye; the hair is light brown under the helm.
9. **Full-field turbulence** - no dead negative space; the whole card is
   agitated air, smoke, and shattered light. She is the earthy, dust-laden
   settling-out of the storm.

## Meanings

**Crowley's descriptive meaning (BoT, court chapter):** Stern and revengeful;
her logic is destructive. She is firm and aggressive, with great practical
wisdom and subtlety in material things. She shows great cleverness and
dexterity in the management of practical affairs, especially where they are
controversial; very adroit in the settlement of controversies. She partakes
of Minerva and Artemis, with a suggestion of the Valkyrie, and represents to
some extent the anger of the Gods.

**Ill-dignified (BoT):** all these qualities are dispersed; she becomes
incoherent, and all her gifts tend to combine into a species of low cunning
whose object is unworthy of the means.

**Yi King (BoT):** the 18th hexagram, "Ku" - "troubles"; for practical and
material matters the most unhappy symbol in the book. All the fine qualities
of Air are weighed down, suppressed, suffocated. People thus characterized
are slow mentally, the prey of constant anxiety, crushed by every kind of
responsibility, especially in family affairs; a parent is usually found in
the aetiology. Line 6 "shows us one who does not serve either king or feudal
lord, but in a lofty spirit prefers to follow his own bent" - explained by
the Princess being "the throne of Spirit," free to throw everything
overboard and blow everything sky high. Such people are rare and appear
often as "Children of misfortune," yet they have chosen aright and in due
season gain their reward.

**Court-theory keynote (BoT, Four Dignitaries):** As Hé final she is the
ultimate issue of the original Energy in its completion, crystallization,
materialization; also its counter-balancing and re-absorption; "the Silence
into which all things return." Permanent and non-existent at once, an audit
of 0=2. No zodiacal attribution; an "elemental" type whose moral qualities
can seem to "lack bite," subdivided by planetary predominance.

**DuQuette keynote:** The earth subelement of Air. When the mind (Ruach) must
be defeated in battle, the Ruach "sends its finest swordswoman into the
field" - a warrior-princess who manifests everything hidden in her lord the
Ace: a Minerva, an Artemis, a Valkyrie. She is the fixation of the volatile,
the materialization of Idea, turning things to stone. Because Princesses do
not rule degrees of the zodiacal year, there are no celebrated-personality
examples for her.

## Observed Harris palette (from the card scan)

`reference/princess-swords-card.jpg`, scan source: esotericmeanings.com
court-cards page. The painting fixes the tone the text only names:
- **Figure robed in green** - a strong olive / emerald green robe is the
  dominant body color, not the "yellows and blues" DuQuette says the suit
  "once was" (line 7812); Air's colors have been darkened by the storm.
- **Smoke / dust base: gray-brown to near-black** - a heavy boiling mass of
  grayish-brown cloud at the bottom third she rises out of.
- **Radiating light: yellow-green / acid yellow** - shattered light-bursts
  behind the figure, sickly warm against the green; DuQuette notes "metallic
  greens" and residual "yellows" (cf. line 7757).
- **Wings / wing-panes: pale gray-white geometric facets** - angular
  translucent panels fanning from the shoulders, cool and hard.
- **Sword: brown / dull steel** - DuQuette plate "Drawn brown sword"; not a
  bright silver blade.
- **Altar: gray stone / silver** - DuQuette plate "Silver altar, smoke," a
  muted stone slab lower in the frame.
- **Hair light brown, eyes blue** (DuQuette plate) - the eye is the one cool
  point; largely hidden under the helm.
- Overall a darkened, agitated Air palette: green figure + gray-brown dust +
  acid yellow-green light + pale geometric wings. Introduce no color with no
  referent in scan or text.
