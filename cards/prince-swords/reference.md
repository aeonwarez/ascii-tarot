# Prince of Swords

Definitive working reference. Synthesizes all project sources; mirrors the
depth of the trump sheets, adapted for a court card (no numeral, elemental
title, Harris scan available from esotericmeanings.com).

## Element ranking (art priorities)

Tier 1 - the card IS these:
1. **The chariot drawn by winged children / arch-fays.** "The chariot which
   bears him suggests (even more closely) geometrical ideas. This chariot is
   drawn by winged children, looking and leaping irresponsibly in any
   direction that takes their fancy; they are not reined, but perfectly
   Capricious." Three winged arch-fairies scatter at the base of the card,
   each hauling a line off in a different direction. They are the card's whole
   thesis: motion without destination. The chariot is easy to move but cannot
   progress "except by accident. This is a perfect picture of the Mind."
2. **Sword up in the right hand, sickle down in the left.** "In his right hand
   is a lifted sword wherewith to create, but in his left hand a sickle, so
   that what he creates he instantly destroys." Create-and-destroy in the same
   figure, one gesture per hand. Sword raised high, sickle held low. This is
   the single most identifying pair of objects.
3. **The Prince himself, armoured in closely-woven mail with a definite
   device.** "The figure of this Prince is clothed with closely woven armour
   adorned with definite device." Seated / standing in the chariot as the one
   held human form amid the scattering fairies. His body is the axis the
   diverging pull-lines radiate from.
4. **The geometric patterns of Air reduced by the Mind.** "The operation of
   his logical mental processes have reduced the Air, which is his element, to
   many diverse geometrical patterns, but in these there is no real plan; they
   are demonstrations of the powers of the Mind without definite purpose." The
   whole field is faceted, crystalline, ruled into triangles and lattices. Air
   made into diagrams that go nowhere.
5. **The radiant child's head / secret crown.** "On the head of this Prince
   is, nevertheless, a child's head radiant, for there is a secret crown in the
   nature of this card; if concentrated, it is exactly Tiphareth." A small
   radiant head crowns him: the hidden solar centre inside all the scattering.

Tier 2 - named and emphasized:
6. **Yellow bubbles enclosing the wings.** DuQuette: "The geometrical wings of
   the Prince and the children are enclosed in bright yellow bubbles - air of
   air." Round luminous cells around each set of wings; the airy medium made
   visible as bright spheres.
7. **Drawn swords and dark clouds / nimbi.** DuQuette symbol list: "Arch
   Fairies winged. Dark clouds, nimbi, drawn swords." Sword blades and cloud
   masses recur through the field, the suit's weather.

Tier 3 - supporting:
8. **The winged angel's head crest.** DuQuette crest: "Winged angel's head."
   Rhymes with the radiant child's head; the airy, disembodied intellect.
9. **The un-reined, capricious motion.** No reins run to the fairies; the
   pull-lines are slack or crossed. The composition must read as diverging, not
   converging: every limb and line points a different way.

Tier 4 - fine detail, as space allows:
10. **The device on the armour.** The "definite device" on the closely-woven
    mail; a small blazon that gives the one figure its single fixed mark
    against the chaos.

## Meanings

**Crowley's character portrait (BoT chapter; the court cards have no separate
divinatory appendix, the chapter description IS the meaning):** purely
intellectual. "Full of ideas and designs which tumble over each other. He is a
mass of fine ideals unrelated to practical effort." Has all the apparatus of
Thought in the highest degree, intensely clever, admirably rational, but
unstable of purpose and in reality indifferent even to his own ideas, "as
knowing that any one of them is just as good as any other." He reduces
everything to unreality by removing its substance and transmuting it to "an
ideal world of ratiocination which is purely formal and out of relation to any
facts." Immensely powerful "because of its complete freedom from settled
principles," able to argue any thesis, "insusceptible of regret or remorse,
glib to quote Scripture aptly and cunningly to support any thesis soever."

**Ill-dignified / the shadow (BoT):** these "elusive and elastic people are of
value only when firmly mastered by creative will fortified by an intelligence
superior to their own." No purchase is to be had upon them. Faddists, devotees
of drink, drugs, humanitarianism, music or religion, wandering "from one cult
or one vice to another, always brilliantly supporting with the fanaticism of a
fixed conviction what is actually no more than the whim of the moment." Easy to
be deceived by: "it is as if an imbecile offered one the dialogues of Plato."

**As a person (DuQuette shortcut):** somebody's birthday card, January 10 to
February 8. When the create-and-destroy mind is given a creative outlet (music,
literature, film), it becomes genius. Named Princes of Swords: Mozart,
Mendelssohn, D. W. Griffith, Federico Fellini, Emanuel Swedenborg, James Dean,
Edgar Allan Poe.

**Vau / the Son:** "the active issue of their union, and its manifestation; the
intellectual image of their union." His action is more enduring than the
Knight's or Queen's. Also "the Dying God, redeeming his Bride in the hour, and
by the virtue, of his murder."

**I Ching correspondence (BoT):** the airy part of Air is the 57th Hexagram,
Sun (the Gentle, Wind), "one of the most difficult figures in the book, on
account of its ambivalence: it means both flexibility and penetration."

## Observed Harris palette (from the card scan)

`reference/prince-swords-card.jpg` (scan source: esotericmeanings.com
court-cards page). The painting fixes what the text only implies:
- **Field: pale silver-white ice**, ruled everywhere into thin crystalline
  facet lines. The whole ground is shattered glass / geometry, not open sky.
- **The Prince: metallic green**, the dominant figure-color. His armour and
  body read deep green, the one saturated mass in a card of pale splinters.
- **Bubbles / sun-disks: bright warm yellow-gold**, large round cells scattered
  behind and around the figure and the fairies - the "air of air" bright
  yellow bubbles made literal. Several read as full yellow suns.
- **Fairy-children: green**, small, winged, tumbling at the base, each pulling
  a slack line off in its own direction.
- **Line-work / facets: fine grey-green and white** ruled lines dividing the
  whole field into triangles and lattices.
- DuQuette's text palette confirms the scan: "Clouds of sharp white ice
  crystals, yellows, and metallic greens dominate this card."
- Border: pale warm frame with a court title band, no numeral plaque.

Map for ANSI: metallic green on the Prince and fairies; bright yellow-gold on
the bubble-suns; pale silver-white and grey-green on the crystalline facet
field; keep no color that has no referent above.
