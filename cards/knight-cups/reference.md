# Knight of Cups

Definitive working reference. Synthesizes all project sources. Follows the
`cards/17-star/reference.md` template (written as the template for all 78
sheets) and the sibling court sheet `cards/knight-wands/reference.md`. NOTE:
this is a COURT card, but unlike Knight of Wands a Harris SCAN now exists in
the repo (`reference/knight-cups-card.jpg`, esotericmeanings.com court-cards
page), so the palette and art priorities below are observed from the painting,
cross-checked against the text.

## Identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Rank / suit | Knight of Cups (Thoth "Knight" = the mounted King figure, the top court rank) |
| Element | Fire of Water (the fiery part of Water) |
| Tetragrammaton | Yod of YHVH (Yod subdivision of the Water suit; the most sublime, original, active part of the Element) |
| Astrology | Rules the Heavens from the 21st degree of Aquarius to the 20th degree of Pisces (Crowley); DuQuette rounds it 20 Aquarius to 20 Pisces, Feb 9 to Mar 10 (last decan of Aquarius + first two decans of Pisces) |
| Natural force | Rain and Springs (Knights = Yod = swift, violent, transient action of the Element); Water's power of solution, the swift passionate attack of rain and springs |
| Zodiacal harmony | Dominates mutable Pisces; the mutable-Pisces "wet blanket" tempers the fiery lord into a placid nature (DuQuette) |
| Original GD titles | "Lord of the Waves and the Waters; The King of the Hosts of the Sea; King of Nymphs or Undines" |
| Rules (small cards) | 7 of Swords; 8 of Cups; 9 of Cups |
| Yi King | 54th hexagram, Kwei Mei (obscure and somewhat sinister; the difficulty of rightly mating opposites, here Fire troubling Water) |
| Central figure | A knight in black winged armour on a leaping white charger, bearing a cup from which a crab issues |

Note on the two zodiacal figures: Crowley's chapter states "the 21st degree of
Aquarius to the 20th degree of Pisces"; DuQuette's attribution box rounds to
"20 Aquarius to 20 Pisces." Both name the same span (last decan of Aquarius
plus the first two decans of Pisces). Cite the exact wording of whichever
source you quote; do not silently pick one number.

Note on the Sagittarius/Rainbow remark: in the general court theory (BoT p.35)
Crowley writes that the watery part of Fire harmonizes with Sagittarius and the
Rainbow. That passage is about the Knight of WANDS (fiery part of Fire, whose
zodiacal span runs into Sagittarius), NOT this card. Do not attach the Rainbow
or Sagittarius to the Knight of Cups; his harmony is Rain and Springs.

## Element ranking (art priorities)

Observed from `reference/knight-cups-card.jpg` and cross-checked against the
text. In the scan the Knight leaps to the LEFT (opposite the Knight of Wands),
his whole world is water, and everything is cool blue-green.

Tier 1 - the card IS these:
1. **The leaping white charger.** "the leaping attitude of his white charger"
   indicates the most active aspect of Water. In the scan the horse is a pale,
   near-white body mid-leap, the single brightest mass on the card, thrusting
   left and up out of the water. Draw it as a LIGHT dithered mass (density ramp
   toward white), the inverse of the Knight of Wands's dark horse. Because
   cells are 1:2, draw the barrel wider than tall.
2. **Water is the whole field, and he rides on / out of it.** Fire of Water
   made literal: the entire ground and background of the scan is churning
   blue-green water, curling waves and eddies. The horse and rider erupt from
   it and are carried on it, exactly as the Knight of Wands rides upon flame.
   This is the #1 environmental read: not a backdrop, the surface.
3. **The winged black armour.** "clothed in black armour furnished with bright
   wings." In the scan the wings read as a large pale, feather-scaled fan
   sweeping up and back behind the rider (the biggest single curve on the
   card). The wings plus the leaping horse together signal the MOST ACTIVE
   aspect of Water. Draw the armour dark, the wings bright.
4. **The cup raised in his right hand, crab issuing.** "In his right hand he
   bears a cup from which issues a crab, the cardinal sign of Water, for
   aggressiveness." In the scan the cup is lifted high to the upper right, a
   warm red/gold vessel (the one warm accent on an all-cool card), with the
   crab shape breaking from its lip. Read the crab even at small scale; it is
   the fiery aggression inside the water.

Tier 2 - named and emphasized:
5. **The peacock totem / peacock-plume of water.** "His totem is the peacock,
   for one of the stigmata of water in its most active form is brilliance."
   DuQuette: Harris's peacock "seems to be fashioned from a plume of pure water
   created by the wake of the Knight's movement." So the peacock is NOT a bird
   perched on the card; it is the brilliant, eyed, fanning wake of water
   trailing the leap. Read the great curling fan behind/below the horse as both
   wave and peacock plume at once.
6. **Fluorescence / brilliance.** "There is here also some reference to the
   phenomena of fluorescence." The active water glints and shimmers. Let the
   water mass carry bright highlight glints (the peacock-eye brilliance), not a
   flat teal fill.
7. **The forward (leftward) leap thrust.** The counter-motion to the Knight of
   Wands. DuQuette: he leaps "in the opposite direction than that of the Knight
   of Wands." Everything (mane, tail, wings, plume-wake) trails the leap. Rake
   the trailing water and feathers the same diagonal.

Tier 3 - supporting:
8. **The sea.** DuQuette lists "Sea" among the symbols. The lower and outer
   field is open water, the King of the Hosts of the Sea in his element.
9. **Mane and tail of the white horse** streaming as part of the wake in
   directive 7, pale against the blue-green.
10. **The crab as texture.** Beyond the cup, the crab's cardinal-Water
    aggression can echo as a small hard glint in the otherwise soft water, the
    one aggressive note ("swift passionate attack of rain and springs").

Tier 4 - fine detail, as space allows:
11. **Blue eyes / fair hair** (DuQuette color note): a small warm-fair highlight
    at the head, cool blue eye glint, if the face is legible at scale.
12. **Scale / feather texture** on the bright wings, dithered so they read as a
    feathered/eyed fan (peacock), not a flat wedge.

## Meanings

**Crowley's court-card description (BoT, canonical):** The person signified is
mostly passive, per the Zodiacal attribution. He is graceful, dilettante, with
the qualities of Venus or a weak Jupiter; amiable in a passive way; quick to
respond to attraction and easily enthusiastic under stimulus, but not very
enduring; exceedingly sensitive to external influence, but with no material
depth of character. Ill dignified he is sensual, idle and untruthful. Yet he
possesses an innocence and purity that are the essence of his nature, though he
is so superficial it is hard to reach that depth. "His name is writ in water."

**Yi King reading (BoT):** The 54th hexagram, Kwei Mei. Singularly obscure and
somewhat sinister; the difficulty of rightly mating opposites such as fire and
water (compare the Queen of Wands, but there Water calms while here Fire makes
trouble). Swiftness and violence ill suit a naturally placid character; rare is
the person who harmonizes these conflicting elements. He tends to mismanage his
affairs, and his mental "civil war" can end in melancholy; the abuse of
stimulants and narcotics may precipitate the catastrophe.

**Keynote (DuQuette):** Fire of Water. A cool contrast to his brother the
Knight of Wands, leaping in the opposite direction. Fire of Water is water in
action: a pelting rain, a gushing spring, and the patient erosion whereby water
dissolves things. "A pretty nice guy. He's just not very deep." The worst you
can call him is "shallow water." Natural innocence and purity, but often not
deep enough for those virtues to manifest as profound nobility. "His name is
writ in water."

**As a person (Crowley / DuQuette):** any court card pictures the person whose
Sun, or rising sign at nativity, falls within its zodiacal attribution. The
Knight of Cups is the birthday card of those born Feb 9 to Mar 10. DuQuette's
example figures span the shallow and the great: Sonny Bono, Mia Farrow, Burt
Reynolds, but also George Washington, Abraham Lincoln, Susan B. Anthony, and
Thomas Edison.

**Knight = Yod role (BoT general remarks):** The Knights represent the powers
of the letter Yod in the Name. They are the most sublime, original, active part
of the Energy of the Element, and for this reason are shown on horseback and in
complete armour. Their action is swift and violent, but transient. In Water the
Knight corresponds to Rain and Springs.

## Quotes for the card page

- "The Knight of Cups represents the fiery part of Water, the swift passionate
  attack of rain and springs; more intimately, Water's power of solution... He
  is clothed in black armour furnished with bright wings which, together with
  the leaping attitude of his white charger, indicates that he represents the
  most active aspect of Water." (BoT, Knight of Cups)
- "In his right hand he bears a cup from which issues a crab, the cardinal sign
  of Water, for aggressiveness. His totem is the peacock, for one of the
  stigmata of water in its most active form is brilliance." (BoT, Knight of Cups)
- "He is amiable in a passive way... but he is not very enduring... His name is
  writ in water." (BoT, Knight of Cups)
- "Harris's version... includes a most beautiful and subtle peacock that seems
  to be fashioned from a plume of pure water created by the wake of the Knight's
  movement." (DuQuette)

## Observed Harris palette (from the card scan)

`reference/knight-cups-card.jpg` (scan source: esotericmeanings.com court-cards
page). The painting confirms and sharpens the text:
- Field: the whole card is COOL. Churning blue-green and teal water fills the
  ground and background, curling into waves and eddies edge to edge. DuQuette:
  "Cool blues and blue-greens dominate this card, as they do the other Cups
  court cards."
- **White charger:** the horse is pale, near-white with grey-blue shadow, the
  brightest and most active mass, leaping to the LEFT.
- **Armour: dark green-black** with a metallic sheen, the rider's body reading
  as a dark silhouette against the pale horse and bright water.
- **Wings: bright, pale feathered** ("furnished with bright wings"), a large
  fan sweeping up and back; doubling as the peacock-plume of water.
- **Cup: warm red / gold**, raised to the upper right, the single warm accent on
  an otherwise all-cool card, with the crab breaking from its lip.
- **Peacock / plume-wake:** blue-green with brilliant eyed highlights, fashioned
  from the water of the Knight's wake, not a separate perched bird.
- Hair: fair; eyes: blue (DuQuette color notes), small warm/cool head accents.
- Border: pale art deco frame; the scan crop shows the water bleeding to the
  edges.

Palette tenor for the .ans: cool teal / blue-green field, pale-white horse,
dark green-black armour, bright wings, one warm red-gold cup. Introduce no
color with no referent in the scan (no fiery reds beyond the cup accent).
