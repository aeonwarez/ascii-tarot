# Queen of Wands

Definitive working reference. Synthesizes all project sources for this court
card; built on the XVII - The Star sheet template, adapted for courts (no
numeral plaque, no Harris scan, elemental title band).

## Element ranking (art priorities)

Tier 1 - the card IS these:
1. **Enthroned queen, seated upon a throne of flame** - "ordered into
   geometrical light by her material power." She is the Water of Fire made
   regal: still, receptive, transmitting. The throne is the single largest
   mass and the compositional stage.
2. **The throne of flame, geometrical below, steady** - "Beneath the throne
   the surging flames are steady." Fire made orderly and calm by her watery
   nature. Rectilinear, faceted flame-geometry under her, not a wild blaze.
3. **The couchant leopard at her side** - "She is attended by a couchant
   leopard upon whose head she lays her hand." A crested/winged leopard
   (DuQuette lists Crest: Winged leopard). Her hand rests on its head; the
   contact reads calm authority over animal fire.
4. **The wand in her left hand, topped with a cone** - "it is topped with a
   cone suggestive of the mysteries of Bacchus." Held in the LEFT hand. The
   cone (fir-cone / thyrsus) is the specific detail, not a plain rod.

Tier 2 - named and emphasized:
5. **Crown topped with the winged globe and rayed with flame** - the crown is
   a specific form: a winged globe crest above, rays of flame around.
6. **Long red-golden hair flowing down** - "Her long red golden hair flows
   down upon her armour of scaled mail." Hair and armour flow together;
   red-gold is the confirmed hair color (DuQuette: Hair red-gold).
7. **Armour of scaled mail** - she is armoured, not merely robed; the hair
   spills over the scales.

Tier 3 - supporting:
8. **Her in-drawn, ecstatic face** - "Her face expresses the ecstasy of one
   whose mind is well in-drawn to the mystery borne beneath her bosom." Eyes
   half-closed, meditative; not outward-facing.
9. **The mystery borne beneath her bosom** - implied gestation / in-drawn
   secret; the reason for the ecstasy. Suggested by pose, not a literal
   object.

Tier 4 - fine detail, as space allows:
10. **Steady, salient flame field** around throne and figure, contrasting the
    Knight's waving flames - here the fire is calmed, translucent, reflective
    (the Rainbow / Sagittarius harmony read from the suit theory).

## Meanings

**Crowley's character description (BoT chapter, canonical):** adaptability,
persistent energy, calm authority which she knows how to use to enhance her
attractiveness. Kindly and generous, but impatient of opposition. Immense
capacity for friendship and for love, but always on her own initiative.

**Pride and its shadow (BoT):** "There is as much pride in this card as in the
Knight, but it lacks the spontaneous nobility which excuses that error. It is
not true pride, but self-complacent vanity and even snobbery." Ill-dignified,
she may brood, come to a wrong decision, and react with great savagery; she
may be easily deceived, then stupid, obstinate, tyrannical; quick to take
offence, harbouring revenge without good cause.

**Crowley's keynote line (BoT):** "when she misses her bite, she breaks her
jaw!" The savage over-reach of the wrongly-energized Queen.

**Yi King (BoT):** 17th hexagram Sui - reflection upon impulse and the even
flow of action; great capacity for lucid conception and steady prosecution of
work, but only at the bidding of some creative mind. A tendency to be fickle,
even disloyal; fits of melancholy cured by intoxication or panic-stricken
fury.

**DuQuette (character read):** the top of the card projects supreme feminine
strength and nobility, fit to be chiseled in stone on a public monument, yet
she is somewhat of a facade and a snob. He likens her to Ayesha in H. Rider
Haggard's "She", the immortal queen "She Who Must Be Obeyed", just but
ruthless and quick to take offence. But she can also be everything the world
needs in a strong woman.

**As a person (court-card use):** anyone whose Sun or rising sign falls in her
zodiacal span (roughly late Pisces into Aries) may carry her traits. She is
someone's birthday card first, an abstraction second.

## Observed Harris palette (from the card scan)

`reference/queen-wands-card.jpg` (esotericmeanings.com court-cards page).
The painting confirms the text and adds the arrangement:
- **Robe / cloak: scarlet to orange-red**, the largest warm mass, spilling from
  the throne down through the frame.
- **Field: vertical orange, red and yellow flame-rays** rising from the base;
  pale geometric facets glow behind the throne (the "geometrical light").
- **Crown: a fan of pale cream / yellow rays** tipped with small flames, above
  a winged-globe crest.
- **Hair: red-gold**, long, veiling the face and spilling over scaled mail.
- **Wand in the LEFT hand**, upright, topped with a pale cone.
- **Leopard: tawny gold with dark spots**, couchant at lower left, her hand
  toward its head.
- **Read:** warmer and steadier than the Knight - the fire here is ordered into
  standing rays rather than a blazing diagonal. DuQuette's tenor holds: "fiery
  reds, yellows, and gold dominate this card, as they do all the Wand court
  cards."
