# Knight of Disks

Definitive working reference. Synthesizes all project sources. Follows the
`cards/17-star/reference.md` template (which was written as the template for
all 78 sheets). NOTE: this is a COURT card, but unlike the other three Knights
a Harris scan of it IS available here (a court-cards page scan), so the palette
section below is observed from the painting, not text-derived.

## Identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Rank / suit | Knight of Disks (Thoth "Knight" = the mounted King figure, the top court rank) |
| Element | Fire of Earth (the fiery part of Earth) |
| Tetragrammaton | Yod of YHVH (Yod subdivision of the Heh-final Earth; the most sublime, original, active part of the Element) |
| Astrology | Rules 21 degrees Leo to 20 degrees Virgo (last decan of Leo + first two decans of Virgo); Aug 12 to Sep 11 |
| Natural force | Mountains (Knights = Yod = swift, violent, transient action of the Element); here also earthquakes, gravitation, and Earth as producer of Life |
| Original GD titles | "The Lord of the Wide and Fertile Land; The King of the Spirits of Earth; King of the Gnomes" |
| Rules (small cards) | 7 of Wands; 8 of Disks; 9 of Disks |
| Yi King | 62nd hexagram, Hsiao Kwo (a "big Khan", trigram of Luna doubled; suggests a bird; the Geomantic Conjunctio, Mercury in Virgo) |
| Central figure | A short, sturdy warrior in heavy plate on a solidly planted shire horse, riding through ripe cornland with a flail and a disk-shield |

Note on the elemental / natural-force reading: in the general court theory
(BoT p.35, p.150) Crowley says the Knight, being Yod, corresponds in each
Element to its most violent original force. In Fire that is the Lightning
flash; in Earth it is Mountains. So the Knight of Disks "the fiery part of
Earth" refers in particular to mountains, earthquakes, and gravitation, but
ALSO to the activity of Earth regarded as the producer of Life. Both readings
are true at once. The zodiacal rulership (21 Leo to 20 Virgo) is a separate
axis and does not "harmonize" with the elemental one; do not collapse them.

Note on the degree figure: BoT states "the 21st degree of Leo to the 20th
degree of Virgo" (line 6595). DuQuette's attribution box heads it "20 Leo to
20 Virgo" (line 7822), the same decan span expressed inclusively. This sheet
locks the BoT wording (21 Leo to 20 Virgo) to match the Knight-of-Wands sheet
convention; the span is identical either way (last decan of Leo plus the first
two decans of Virgo).

## Element ranking (art priorities)

Tier 1 - the card IS these:
1. **The heavy shire horse, solidly planted on all four feet.** "A shire
   horse, solidly planted on all four feet, as was not the case with the other
   Knights." This is the single read that separates this Knight from his three
   brothers: their horses leap or rear, his stands still on the ground. Draw
   the whole mass low, wide, and rooted, a dark dithered volume, all four legs
   down. DuQuette: the workhorse "seems more concerned with eyeing the lush
   grass than with conveying his rider." Weight, not motion, is the thesis.
2. **The fertile land he rides through.** "He rides through the fertile land;
   even the distant hills are cultivated fields." The ground is not a backdrop,
   it is the subject: ripe cornland foreground, cultivated hills receding.
   Fire of Earth made literal as agriculture. Fill the lower and middle field
   with ripe wheat / corn, the horizon hills striped as ploughed rows.
3. **The short, sturdy warrior in complete plate.** "This warrior is short and
   sturdy in type. He is clothed in great solidity of plate armour." Not the
   tall violent Knight of Wands; a stocky farmer-king. Heavy dithered plate,
   proportioned squat and grounded, drawn ON TOP of the horse and field.
4. **The raised / thrown-back helmet.** "his helmet, which is crested with the
   head of a stag, is thrown back, for at the moment his function is entirely
   confined to the production of food." He gazes at the fields "as if in
   contemplation of harvest, not battle" (DuQuette). The open visor is the
   whole psychology of the card: he is off duty from war, at work on food.

Tier 2 - named and emphasized:
5. **The winged stag's-head crest.** "crested with the head of a stag"
   (DuQuette: "Winged stag's head"). The stag doubles the theme of the fertile
   wild land. Read the antlered crest even at small scale above the thrown-back
   helm.
6. **The flail.** "For this reason he is armed with a flail." Harris even
   borrowed a real flail to paint it (letter to Crowley, Nov 3 1939: "a lovely
   instrument of solid wood. Most difficult to manage"). DuQuette: it "dangles
   near the grasses, suggesting the thrashing of wheat rather than the
   thrashing of heads." A threshing tool, not a weapon: the two-part hinged
   flail hanging low toward the grain.
7. **The solid disk / shield.** "The disk which he bears, moreover, is very
   solid; it represents nutrition." DuQuette: "his shield is a disk that could
   double as a dish that could hold enough food to feed a village." A heavy
   round disk-shield, read as a coin AND a serving dish of plenty.

Tier 3 - supporting:
8. **The scepter with the Hexagram of the Zelator Adeptus Minor** (DuQuette
   symbol note). A grade-hexagram device; optional detail on the disk or a
   secondary implement where space allows.
9. **The smouldering, not blazing, fire.** "their fire is the smouldering fire
   of the process of growth." The Fire of this card is heat of germination and
   ripening, not flame. Palette warmth (golden ripe corn, warm brown) carries
   the fire, NOT literal flame-tongues. Do not draw open fire; that is the
   Knight of Wands, not this one.
10. **The cultivated distant hills.** The horizon "even the distant hills are
    cultivated fields" - striped ploughland receding, reinforcing the whole
    frame as worked, fertile Earth.

Tier 4 - fine detail, as space allows:
11. **The horse eyeing the grass** (DuQuette). A tilt of the horse's head down
    toward the corn, so the mount reads placid and grazing, not martial.
12. **Plate / scale texture** on the heavy armour, dithered so it reads solid
    metal weight, not a flat body.

## Meanings

**Crowley's court-card description (BoT, canonical):** Those whom he symbolizes
tend to be dull, heavy, and preoccupied with material things. They are
laborious and patient, but have little intellectual grasp even of matters that
concern them most closely; their success is due to instinct, to imitation of
Nature. They lack initiative; their fire is the smouldering fire of the process
of growth. Ill-dignified: hopelessly stupid, slavish, incapable of foresight,
churlish, surly, jealous in a dull way, always irritably meddling in petty
matters and spoiling whatever comes their way.

**Yi King reading (BoT):** The 62nd hexagram, Hsiao Kwo, the fiery part of
Earth. A "big Khan", the trigram of Luna with each line doubled; also the
Geomantic figure Conjunctio, Mercury in Virgo, corresponding closely to the
Fire-of-Earth attribution. To the Chinese sages the shape suggested a bird, so
the meaning is modified by frivolous, irresponsible human influence. But
Mercury in Virgo is Intelligence and creative Idea applied to Agriculture, in
harmony with the Ten of Disks. In the happiest cases: romance and imagination;
the perils are overweening ambition, the pursuit of Ignis Fatuus, superstition,
and idle dreaming in "sons of the soil" (Crowley names Thomas Hardy's
portraits of the type).

**Keynote (DuQuette):** Fire of Earth. The Knight of Disks is unique among his
brother Knights: shortest in stature, helmet fully raised, gazing at fertile
fields as if contemplating harvest, not battle, his flail dangling near the
grasses to thresh wheat rather than heads, his shield a disk that could double
as a dish to feed a village. His function "is entirely confined to the
production of food." He keeps his nose to the grindstone and takes little
interest in intellectual musings or the finer aspects of culture. If
ill-aspected he makes ignorance a virtue and takes obstinate pride in his own
lack of sophistication.

**As a person (Crowley / DuQuette):** any court card pictures the person whose
Sun, or rising sign at nativity, falls within its zodiacal attribution. The
Knight of Disks is the birthday card of those born roughly Aug 12 to Sep 11.
DuQuette notes both brilliant natives of the span (Napoleon Bonaparte,
Cardinal Richelieu, Louis XIV, Bill Clinton, Madonna, H. P. Lovecraft) and
example figures who fit the plainer character (Davy Crockett, Annie Oakley,
Buck Owens, Porter Wagoner, Patsy Cline, Jim Reeves).

**Knight = Yod role (BoT general remarks):** The Knights represent the powers
of the letter Yod in the Name. They are the most sublime, original, active part
of the Energy of the Element, and for this reason are shown on horseback and in
complete armour. Their action is swift and violent, but transient. In Earth the
Knight corresponds to Mountains (in Fire, the Lightning flash; in Water, Rain
and Springs; in Air, Wind).

## Quotes for the card page

- "The Knight of Disks represents the fiery part of Earth, and refers in
  particular to the phenomena of mountains, earthquakes, and gravitation; but
  it also represents the activity of Earth regarded as the producer of Life."
  (BoT, Knight of Disks)
- "His helmet, which is crested with the head of a stag, is thrown back, for
  at the moment his function is entirely confined to the production of food.
  For this reason he is armed with a flail... These characteristics are borne
  out by his horse; a shire horse, solidly planted on all four feet, as was not
  the case with the other Knights. He rides through the fertile land; even the
  distant hills are cultivated fields." (BoT, Knight of Disks)
- "Someone has lent me a genuine flail. It is like this... a lovely instrument
  of solid wood. Most difficult to manage." (Harris to Crowley, Nov 3 1939)
- "His flail dangles near the grasses, suggesting the thrashing of wheat rather
  than the thrashing of heads, and his shield is a disk that could double as a
  dish that could hold enough food to feed a village." (DuQuette)

## Observed Harris palette (from the card scan)

`reference/knight-disks-card.jpg` (esotericmeanings.com court-cards page).
Unlike the other three Knights, a court scan IS available for this card, so
this palette is observed from the painting and cross-checked with the color
words in the text:
- **Armour: dark, near-black burnished plate** with cool steel highlights, not
  the golden armour of the Wand court. The knight reads as a heavy dark mass
  from behind.
- **Horse: rich reddish / chestnut brown**, a broad-chested heavy draught
  animal, pale flaxen mane and tail; solidly standing.
- **Sky: molten orange-into-yellow**, a low warm sun/disk behind the figure,
  the "smouldering fire of growth" carried as this glow rather than as flame.
- **Fields / grasses: golden ripe corn and green blades** in the foreground and
  up the sides, wheat-heads catching the light.
- **Distant hills: brown-grey cultivated slopes** under the warm sky.
- **Cloak / mantle: deep red-brown**, hanging heavy at the horse's flank.
- **Disk-shield: dark disk with a lighter rim / boss**, held at the knight's
  back shoulder.
- **Overall tenor:** DuQuette, "Rich browns, greens, and golden yellow dominate
  this card." Warm earth palette, no scarlet flame; the fire is sunlight and
  ripe grain.
