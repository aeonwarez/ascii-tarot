# XVI - The Tower (War)

Definitive working reference. Synthesizes the project sources - Crowley's *Book of
Thoth* and DuQuette's *Understanding...Thoth Tarot* - against the Harris scan. No
esotericmeanings.com tutorial exists for this card in `reference/`, so the Upright /
Reversed readings below are derived from the BoT divinatory appendix, the title-page
verse, and DuQuette.

## Identity

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Number / name | Atu XVI - The Tower, subtitled WAR. Ancient title "The House of God" (or "Destruction of the House of God"), retained only because of AL I:57; Crowley's footnote: "Otherwise, it might have been called War." DuQuette title: "The Lord of the Hosts of the Mighty." |
| Esoteric figure | The EYE OF HORUS / EYE OF SHIVA opening at the top of the card, on whose opening the Universe is destroyed; beneath it the fortress blasted NOT by lightning from heaven but by flame belching upward from the MOUTH of the underworld. Shiva dancing on the bodies of his devotees. |

| Tree of Life | Path 27, Netzach (Victory) -> Hod (Splendour). The horizontal "reciprocal" path across the lower face of the Tree; the wall between feeling and intellect broken open. |

## Element ranking (BoT p.108-109 + DuQuette Atu XVI + the Harris scan)

Tier 1 - the card IS these:
1. **The Eye of Horus / Eye of Shiva** - "the dominating feature of this card." A
   great lidded eye, gold rim and red iris, at the TOP on the axis, throwing straight
   hard rays out across the whole field and a zigzag spark down into the structure.
   Its opening destroys the Universe. Draw it ON TOP of the rays; it is the one
   bilaterally symmetric object on the card, so it anchors col 23.
2. **The blasted, burning Tower** - an angular ochre/yellow fortress left of centre,
   tilting and coming apart into flat cubist facets, a dark arched GATE and small
   black windows still legible in it, sheets of flame tearing through. The House of
   God in the act of destruction.
3. **The jaws of Dis at the base** - the MOUTH (Pe' made literal), an enormous gaping
   maw of banded red/orange/yellow sheets sweeping across the bottom third and
   opening upward from the right, belching flame at the roots of the structure and
   swallowing what falls. Harris's great reversal: the blast comes from BELOW.
4. **The broken figures falling headlong** - the garrison thrown from the tower,
   tumbling down-right into the mouth. "It will be noticed that they have lost their
   human shape. They have become mere geometrical expressions." Pale grey angular
   manikins, jointed like drafting figures, not people.

Tier 2 - named and emphasized:
5. **The Dove with the olive branch (upper left)** - pale, descending on the left,
   bathed in the effulgence of the Eye. The Will to Live, the feminine impulse. One
   half of "there is the dove, and there is the serpent. Choose ye well!"
6. **The Serpent, Lion-Serpent Xnoubis / Abraxas (upper right)** - a lion-headed
   serpent set in a radiant gold solar disc top-right, its coiled body descending
   left. The Will to Die, the masculine impulse. The other half of the choice; the
   pair together are "love under will."
7. **The sheets of flame and the cubist shard-field** - the whole background split
   into hard-edged wedges of fire and black void radiating from the Eye; the
   destruction of the old-established Aeon by lightning, flames, engines of war.
   Straight lines, no curves except the mouth.

Tier 3 - atmosphere / as space allows:
8. **The black wedge voids** - Harris's flat black shards between the flames. The
   Nothingness that is the ultimate reality; every manifestation, however glorious,
   is a stain.
9. **The collapse diagonal** - the whole composition falls from upper left to lower
   right, into the mouth. The Eye is centred; nothing else is. Resist symmetry.
10. **The Mars heat held to the end** - keep every value in the scarlet/orange/ochre
    band; the only relief is the grey of the dove and the falling figures.

## ASCII treatment notes (to prove in drafts)

- NOT a hieratic-frontal card. Only the EYE is bilaterally symmetric; place it with
  the mirror helpers about `AXIS = 23.0` and let everything else be asymmetric by
  design. Verify with `--axis`: the eye's pupil on col 23, the tower leaning left of
  it, the falling figures and the mouth's opening right of it.
- Cells are 1:2 - draw the Eye ~2:1 wider than tall (a lidded almond, not a circle),
  and give the mouth's banded sheets a shallow, wide sweep rather than a tall arc.
- The single most important read: a huge open eye at the top raying fire down onto a
  broken burning tower, figures falling out of it into a gaping mouth at the bottom.
- Rays are the texture of this card: straight `/ \ | _ ' .` fans from the eye, hard
  wedge edges, flat black masses. Dither the flame sheets for volume but keep the
  shard EDGES crisp; the painting is cubist, not smoky.
- The tower must still read as architecture at 47 wide: hold the dark arched gate and
  two or three black windows even as the walls fracture.
- Palette: this is the hottest `.ans` of the run - scarlet, vermilion, orange,
  ochre-gold on black. Deliberately unlike the Star's blues, the Moon's indigo, and
  Art's rainbow. Grey only for the dove and the falling manikins.

## Meanings

**Crowley divinatory (BoT appendix, Atu XVI):** "Quarrel, combat, danger, ruin,
destruction of plans, sudden death, escape from prison."

**Title-page verse (BoT / DuQuette):** "Break down the fortress of thine Individual
Self, that thy Truth may spring free from the ruins!"

**Upright (derived):** The necessary shattering. A structure you built to keep
yourself safe is being taken apart, and the taking apart is the event; sudden,
external, unarguable. Old arrangements, plans, and defences collapse at once rather
than by degrees. Read it with Crowley's double sense: the destruction of the garrison
is also their emancipation from the prison of organized life that was confining them,
and it was their unwisdom to cling to it. Escape from prison, breakthrough, the wall
between Netzach and Hod broken open, the ground cleared for Atu XX. War, Mars energy,
conflict met head on rather than managed.

**Reversed / lower expression:** Quarrel and combat for their own sake; destruction
chosen as a style rather than accepted as a passage. Clinging to the fortress while
it burns; treating the loss as final ("apparent death or destruction, but such
interpretation is illusion"). Danger, ruin, plans wrecked by one's own detonation;
the Shiva doctrine misread as nihilism instead of as annihilation in service of
perfection. Rebuilding the same tower on the same ground.

**BoT texture:** Attributed to Pe', a mouth; it refers to Mars. "In its simplest
interpretation it refers to the manifestation of cosmic energy in its grossest form.
The picture shows the destruction of existing material by fire." Preface to Atu XX,
the Last Judgment, the Coming of a New Aeon; it indicates the quintessential quality
of the Lord of the Aeon. At the bottom, the destruction of the old-established Aeon
by lightning, flames, engines of war; the jaws of Dis belching flame at the roots of
the structure; falling from the tower the broken figures of the garrison, who have
lost their human shape and become mere geometrical expressions. The second and
totally different reading is Shiva's: ultimate reality is Nothingness, so all
manifestations are stains and all existing things must be annihilated; the
destruction of the garrison is their emancipation. "Magical symbols must always be
understood in a double sense, each contradictory of the other." AL I:57 is quoted
direct: "Love is the law, love under will... There is the dove, and there is the
serpent. Choose ye well! He, my prophet, hath chosen, knowing the law of the
fortress, and the great mystery of the House of God." The dominating feature is the
Eye of Horus, also the Eye of Shiva, on whose opening the Universe is destroyed;
there is a further technical meaning reserved to the Eleventh degree of the O.T.O.
Bathed in the effulgence of that Eye are the Dove bearing an olive branch and the
Serpent, portrayed as the Lion-Serpent Xnoubis or Abraxas: the two forms of desire,
the Will to Live and the Will to Die, feminine and masculine, not incompatible once
life and death are understood (see Atu XIII) as phases of a single manifestation of
energy. The dove-and-serpent symbol first appears in Atu V and recurs here.

**DuQuette:** "The Lord of the Hosts of the Mighty," planetary trump of Mars, Pe'
(mouth), Path 27 Netzach to Hod, colours scarlet / red / Venetian red / bright red
rayed azure or emerald. The Tower is phase one of a construction project: clear the
land with smoke-belching bulldozers, photograph it, and you have a picture of a
catastrophe; two years later there is a building. Atu XVI is phase one, Atu XX the
Aeon is phase two. "The Tower is not the end of the world; it just looks like the end
of the world." Two features are unique to Harris: the tower is blasted from BELOW by
flames belching from the mouth of the underworld (the letter Pe' means mouth), a most
significant reverse of the traditional lightning-from-heaven; and the large eye at
the top radiating the zigzag that may be the spark that ignited those flames. On the
eye's O.T.O. secret: the eyes refer to body openings and the psychic centres linked
to them, which does not help much with the trump's general meaning, whereas the Eye
of Shiva does - Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva create, sustain, and destroy, and Shiva
need only open his eye. The citizen of the Aeon of Horus is no longer to fear the
natural processes depicted here. Harris to Crowley, date uncertain: "The House of God
appears to me as a vortex not a mouth, or is it yours which can't be filled by mortal
effort try as you may."

## Expected Harris palette (scan at reference/16-tower-card.jpg)

- Eye: top centre, gold/ochre lid rim, ORANGE iris ring, dark red pupil ring, black
  centre; straight rays fanning out in orange, vermilion, and black wedges
- Tower: ochre / pale yellow walls with red and black facet shadows, dark arched gate
  lower left of it, small black windows, a red roundel on the wall
- Falling figures: pale GREY / white jointed manikins, black outlines, centre-right
- Dove: pale cream-pink with an olive sprig, upper left, on a black crescent field
- Serpent: gold radiant SUN-DISC upper right, lion-headed serpent in orange and ochre
  coiling down-left out of it
- Mouth of Dis: the bottom third, long banded sheets of RED / ORANGE / YELLOW curving
  as a great maw, dark serpentine tongue at the right
- Ground: black voids between everything; no blue, no green anywhere
- Frame: pale warm gray art deco; `XVI` plaque top, "The Tower" band with the Pe' and
  Mars glyphs flanking
