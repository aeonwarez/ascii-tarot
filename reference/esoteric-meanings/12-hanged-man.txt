(ii) Overview

A card like a Baptism in the spiritual sense: we are reborn with a higher perspective.
Crowley insists it has NOTHING to do with religious sacrifice, sin, or debt. "The descent
of light into darkness in order to redeem it" (BoT p96); "a baptism which is also a death";
"annihilation of the self into the beloved." He calls the idea of sacrifice a WRONG idea,
an evil legacy of the old Aeon, like the appendix in the body; and "redemption" a bad word
because it implies a debt. This is a fundamental break from the Waite/Osirian card.

(iii) Historical variations

Waite: left leg crossed, free foot points EAST (we CONSCIOUSLY seek the learning). Marseille
and Thoth: right leg crossed, free foot points WEST (we UNCONSCIOUSLY stumble on it; the
carnal/left side). Crowley removed the wooden gallows of earlier decks (suffering/punishment
of a previous Aeon) and hangs the man from an ANKH (purity of spirit, white light of Kether).
His crossed legs form a figure 4 like the Emperor (Aries): a will directed outward — here
turned toward the spiritual/emotional (water) realm.

(iv) The Tree of Life

Path 23, between Geburah and Hod, directly OPPOSITE the Wheel of Fortune. On the Pillar of
Severity — restriction is inherent. Geburah (Mars): man's finite energy; Mars destroying the
old so the new can form; old patterns and habits lost. Hod (intellect): here it deceives him
into feeling a victim of circumstance, blotting out spirituality for a mundane existence
(hence the bent leg west, unconscious). The lower mind is transformed; life is illuminated by
the light of Kether once Geburah has destroyed what held him back.

(v) Hebrew and elemental attribution

MEM = the ocean / water; one of the three MOTHER LETTERS (value 40). Attributed to WATER, so
the card has a deceptive quality and is easily misinterpreted. Water = the sea of Binah, the
field of potential experience available in a human body. Trinity of mother letters: the Fool
(Aleph, air), the Hanged Man (Mem, water), the Aeon (Shin, fire).

(vi) Symbolism

Theme = SELF SACRIFICE, which Crowley opposed; the card represents man's fear of his own
limitations. Nails in the hands/feet echo the crucifixion (a dying-god Aeon he moves away
from).
Ankh = life; connected to the letter TAU (Cross), tied to the Universe card and the
completion of the Great Work; the 5th power of the sphinx, "to GO" (after to KNOW, to WILL,
to DARE, to KEEP SILENT). Tau glyph in the lower left corner.
Serpent coiled around the foot = linked to the Ankh and to Chokmah (coiled twice = 2nd
sephira), the wisdom/will of God binding the man's foot to the source of life; Chokmah's
duality carries both life and death.
Light of Kether = top of card, white; GREEN air below it = Venus and LOVE (God's infinite
love for creation; every experience an act of love). Green disks at the terminations of the
limbs and head = Grace/Love (Venus).
Body shape = a triangle (Holy Trinity) surmounted by a cross (matter): light entering
darkness. The man connects Kether and Malkuth; his head sits in the centre of the triangle
formed by his hands and torso (like the eye in the pyramid — Enlightenment).
Nails shaped like the letter Vau (Vav) = links him with the Hierophant, a transmitter of
spirituality; Vau relates to Taurus, the action of Will. So he acts out of LOVE following a
spiritual impulse — moving away from sacrifice.
Serpent in Malkuth (below) = black flecked gold (the Princess/lowest colour scale, the lowest
form of creation), contained within a BLACK womb-shape (the black waters of Binah that give
birth to form). Coiled 4 times (structure/manifestation) = the serpent of new life stirring.
18 rays = from the man's head down into the womb; 18 = Chai ("LIFE" in Hebrew): spiritual
light brought into the darkness of matter. A key feature.
Grid of small dark squares (background) = the Enochian Elemental Tablets; the man's crossed
legs (the cross of spirit) sit within a dark square, combining the 4 elements of life in
Malkuth.

(vii) Colours

GD / DuQuette scale: Deep Blue; Sea-green; Deep Olive Green; White flecked purple. Green
(Venus) dominant in the air above the water; white Kether light at top; dark/black womb-waters
below.

(viii) The Lust-to-Hanged-Man transition

Lust (Chesed-Geburah) holds qualities of both the Hanged Man (Geburah, restricting) and the
Wheel of Fortune (Chesed, building). The Hanged Man channels Lust's energy to the highest
good, bridging spirit and matter — e.g. teaching meditation / spirituality altruistically
because it is one's greatest passion. It is the WILLING sacrifice of the lower desires for a
spiritual life, and letting go of guilt/remorse over the past — not negative religious
self-sacrifice.

(ix) Divinatory meanings

The debt man feels to God is an illusion: we are made by him, carry our own divinity and
purpose. We can only be who we were made to be.
Upright: (1) the opportunity to redeem a situation that has turned sour, to put things right
(light into darkness); (2) watery/emotional nature — repair of an emotional or spiritual
transgression; emotions should flow like water; (3) losing emotional ties to guilt from the
past; (4) helping people spiritually for the highest good, giving; (5) freedom from guilt or
blame (a previous-Aeon concept); (6) giving up something lesser for something more fulfilling.
Reversed: self-punishment and guilt about the past; detrimental self-sacrifice when
unnecessary; someone manipulating spirituality for personal gain; a life being turned upside
down; someone who has waited too long, scared to change; sometimes non-action lets better
options emerge.

Title-verse (BoT / DuQuette): "Let not the water whereon thou journeyest wet thee! And, being
come to shore, plant thou the Vine and rejoice without shame."
